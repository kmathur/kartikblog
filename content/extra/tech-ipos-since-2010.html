Tech IPOs since 2010

Every technology company that listed on a US stock exchange (Nasdaq or NYSE) from 2010 on and still trades there today, newest first. Each row compares the first-day opening price with the closing price on Nov 13, 2026, and growth shows what $100 put in at the open is worth now.

How to read this

Open

The price of the first trade on the first day of trading. Where a stock has split since, the price as it traded that day is on top and the split-adjusted figure sits under it.

Growth

What $100 invested at the first-day open is worth at the latest close. A gain shows as a multiple of the money put in: 2x means the $100 is now $200. A loss shows as a negative percentage: −50% means the $100 is now $50. The dollar figure under each one is the value today. It uses the split-adjusted open and does not count dividends.

What counts as tech

Companies that Nasdaq or Yahoo Finance classify as technology: software, semiconductors, hardware, IT services, internet platforms, e-commerce, online gaming, health IT and solar. A short hand-picked set of fintech and marketplace names that data providers file elsewhere is added, such as Airbnb, Coinbase, Robinhood and Affirm. Carmakers such as Tesla and Rivian are left out.

Avg / year

The steady yearly rate that turns the opening price into today's price over the time the stock has been listed, also called the compound annual growth rate. Meta's 15.3x over 14.5 years works out to +20.7% a year. Listings under a year old show a dash, because stretching a few months into a yearly rate gives misleading numbers.

US listings

Every row trades on Nasdaq, the NYSE or NYSE American. Of the IPOs and direct listings, 294 are run from the United States and 179 are foreign companies that list here directly or through depositary shares; their home country sits beside the ticker, and the country filter hides them. Home country is where the company is headquartered, not where it is registered.

Listing types

IPOs and direct listings are shown by default. SPAC mergers are optional because their opening price is the first trade on record for that listing, often the blank-check shell near $10, so the comparison means something different.

What is missing

Companies that were acquired, taken private or delisted have no current price; the best-known are listed below. Spin-offs (Keysight, HPE, Sandisk), moves up from over-the-counter markets, 20 micro-caps that listed as something else and later pivoted to tech, and 8 with unusable price history are excluded. Match Group and Nebius (formerly Yandex) are out because their share history no longer lines up with their IPOs.

Sources

IPO dates from the Nasdaq IPO calendar. Current listings and sectors from the Nasdaq stock screener. Daily prices, splits and industries from Yahoo Finance. Micro-cap rows with heavy reverse splits are the least reliable; a dagger marks rows where the price feed begins a few days after the IPO.
